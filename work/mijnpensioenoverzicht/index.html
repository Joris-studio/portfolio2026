Mijnpensioenoverzicht

Detail only when you ask for it

Half of the visitors to Mijnpensioenoverzicht.nl came on mobile. Yet the platform made a simple question complicated: how much will I get each month? I traced separate usability problems back to one structural problem and designed a different order: answer first, explanation second.

Client
Mijnpensioenoverzicht.nl
Role
Lead designer
Period
Q4, via Frog Design
My contribution
Analysis, journeys, mobile prototype

Five pain points. One pattern.

MPO came with five pain points from user research. Laid side by side, the screens kept showing the same thing: the platform was organised around information, while visitors arrived with a concrete question or task.

Amounts, explanation and next steps were mixed together, and visitors had to connect ages, amounts and providers themselves. Fixing the problems one by one would not help while the structure stayed the same.

From my FigJam notes on the existing screens

“This pie chart is experienced as confusing and lacks context. It also pushes the CTAs and the overview below the fold.”

Key actions disappeared under explanation.

“These info icons and blocks of text lack context.”

Lots of information, no clear coherence.

“This page is experienced as ‘annoying’. That comes from the lack of structure.”

The problem sat in the structure, not in one element.

Room to change

Visitors wanted to know their monthly amount quickly. Rebuilding the platform was out of scope, so I worked with the building blocks that were there.

One constraint weighed heavily: gross and net could not be set per amount. That made what you show, and when, even more important. To verify

My trade-off: no new features. Order and pace the information that was already there.

Answer first, then explanation

I ordered the experience in three layers, each tied to a question visitors arrive with. Important context stays available. It just doesn’t need to be on screen at the same time.

1 · Insight

“How much pension will I get?”

2 · Understanding

“How does that amount come about?”

3 · Control

“What can or must I do now?”

The three layers in the prototype

1 · Insight

What will I get each month?

The overview opens with one sentence and one amount. The sentence holds the age; the amount is net per month. That is the number someone can act on. Everything else on the page supports that answer instead of crowding it.

Look at: the age inside the sentence, and the one net amount below it.

2 · Understanding

How do AOW and my pensions add up?

AOW and pensions sit above the amount as two blocks, with a plus sign between them, so the sum is visible without reading a table. Tap a block for the detail: the amount per provider, and whether you choose the start date yourself.

Look at: ‘kies jij’ or ‘staat vast’ under each provider.

3 · Control

What can I do, and where do I learn more?

Explanation sits with the topic it explains. Unsure about your AOW age? The AOW block links straight to a short answer, with the option to read on. Not everything can be arranged on MPO; for things like the start date, MPO refers visitors to their pension provider, so that next step is visible.

Look at: ‘Ok, duidelijk’ for done, ‘Leg eens uit?’ for more.

One age at a time

The old page listed amounts per age further down, so you had to work out what changed when. I replaced it with an age choice in years and months: pick one age, see the situation that belongs to it, and open the details if you want more.

In FigJam I noted: making the age dynamic shows there are choices.

Bringing stakeholders along

A different order touches more than one screen. Existing interfaces, regulation and technical possibilities shaped how much room there was. Those limits were real, and the people guarding them knew them better than I did.

What helped was making it concrete. I wrote journeys from what the visitor does. With the prototype we could see together what the new order meant, screen by screen.

  1. I log in with DigiD
  2. I read that my pensions are loaded and say whether I live together
  3. I see AOW and pension apart, and how they add up
  4. I try different ages and see my amount change
  5. I open the pension block, see some pots at zero, and read why

What the tests showed, and what they don’t

Shown

In the user test, all participants understood the prototype. The findings suggested the new structure helped them understand amounts and find their way, which gave stakeholders confidence to discuss further improvements to the structure.

Not claimed

Effects after implementation. This case covers the prototype phase; no figures after launch are shown.

Still open

The exact wording of the gross and net constraint. To verify.

Reflection

The most important change was in the order: first an answer to the question someone arrives with, then the explanation they need to move on.
