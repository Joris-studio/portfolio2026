A fail says what happened, not what to change

Many candidates returned to their next theory exam without changing how they prepared. A result tells them that it went wrong. It does not help them understand why, or what to do differently next time.

CBR wanted to help candidates break that pattern, starting with a better understanding of why they failed.

The plan grows while you answer

The plan is not a report at the end. It sits at the bottom of every screen from the first question. Each answer adds to it or makes it more personal, so candidates can see that their reflection leads somewhere.

From questions to a plan, in three steps

1 · Reflect

Short questions in four chapters

The questions are grouped in four chapters, starting with the exam day itself. Answers are short, recognisable situations. Where more than one applies, candidates pick several.

Look at: the chapter progress at the top, and ‘Kies alles wat past’ under the question.

2 · Check

Candidates confirm the picture

Along the way, the flow shows what it sees so far and asks whether that is right. Each observation gets Klopt, Deels or Klopt niet, and that answer sets how much it counts in the plan.

Look at: the line under the heading, which explains why the answer matters.

3 · Plan

A plan to act on

The plan opens with a time frame: in this example, an exam in six weeks at four hours a week. It then shows what the reflection found and whether the candidate recognised it, what already went well, and how to approach studying.

Look at: ‘Dit herken je zelf’ above each insight, and the option to add a tip of your own.
